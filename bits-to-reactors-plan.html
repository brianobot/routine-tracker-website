Notes & reality checks

Three books, fully read, instead of scattered chapters

John (21 chapters), Philippians (4) and James (5) — 30 chapters total, averaging about 1.25/day with a few 2-chapter days to keep slack. All three finish complete by Oct 31 rather than leaving a wide, shallow pass across the whole Bible that covers more ground but finishes nothing. John for the gospel narrative, then two short, practical epistles to close the month.

50 push-ups every day, the rest rotates

Push-ups are the one constant. Alongside them: a brisk walk on Mon/Wed/Fri-pattern days, squats and plank on the alternating strength days, and a lighter stretch-and-walk recovery set on the three review Sundays — the same logic as the learning review days, just for the body instead of the courses.

This is a genuinely intense pace

74 learning nodes in 21 active study days averages 3.5/day, peaking at 4, on top of LinkedIn, X, scripture, a workout and three habit checks on every card below. If each node runs 25–40 minutes, that's 1.5–2.5 hours of focused study most days before any of the rest. It's load-balanced across all three courses so none sits untouched for a week, but it has no slack built in — fall a day behind and the next day absorbs it. If that turns out to be too much, the honest fix is dropping one track's end date into November, not silently skipping nodes. Sleep by 10:30pm is actually load-bearing here, not just a habit to tick: a 3.5-node, six-growth-task, workout-and-reading day doesn't work on a sleep deficit.

November onward — the Nuclear MPhil prep track

Strict order, unlike the sprint. Course 2 extends course 1's own results, course 5 reuses course 1 and 2's machinery, and the capstones chain all the way to Reactor Physics — skipping ahead means hitting a cross-reference to a tool you never built.

Start with Calculus, even though it isn't numbered

It's not one of the 14 NE-track courses, but it's the explicitly-declared prerequisite for Linear Algebra onward (31 nodes, 0 done). Clear it first, in November, before Course 1.

Pacing once you're in it

These nodes are paper-and-pencil proofs, not code quests — budget more per node than the low-level courses. Aim for 4–5 nodes a week (roughly one on most weekdays, weekends free or catch-up), not one a day every day. At that rate Calculus + Courses 1–8 (271 nodes, everything already built) run about a year. That's fine — this track was never meant to race the October sprint.

Courses 9–14 are still being built

Nuclear Thermal-Hydraulics (course 9) is about half-baked right now; Nuclear Materials, Fuel Cycle/Waste, Nuclear Safety, Computational Reactor Modelling and Advanced Fission & Fusion (10–14) don't exist yet. At 4–5 nodes/week you won't reach course 9 for months — by then it should be finished. Ask me to pick building back up whenever you want the runway kept ahead of you.

Why this shape

Interchange freely in the sprint

Python, Rust and algo-trading are three independent courses — algo-trading only assumes you can already read both languages, not that you've finished either low-level course. Swap which one you do on a given day, bank two algo-trading nodes and skip a day, whatever — the weekly total is what matters, not the day-by-day order.

Don't interchange the Masters track

It's one long derivation chain: la-capstone's toy k=1.5 gets a real answer from Reactor Physics eight courses later. Reordering it isn't variety, it's working ahead of tools you haven't built yet.

Review days aren't rest days

Three Sundays in the sprint carry no new nodes — reopen the dragon/owl on anything shaky, redo a missed quiz, or just reread a node you rushed. Complacency creeps in on the 1-new-topic-a-day treadmill, not on a harder day.

The daily count is already balanced

74 nodes across three tracks in 21 active days works out to 2–4 a day, weighted by how many nodes each track still has left so all three land on Oct 31 together. If a day runs long, push the extra node to the next day rather than cramming — the allocation has no spare days to recover one, so slipping costs you later, not nothing.

Growth tasks run every day, including review Sundays

Learning nodes pause on review days; LinkedIn and X don't — an audience doesn't wait for your study schedule. The six daily growth tasks (connect, engage, clear invites on LinkedIn; post, follow, reply on X) are the same template all 24 days, separate from the course checklist, so you can tell at a glance what's "new learning" versus "keep the pipeline moving."

LinkedIn's target is the one that may not close by Oct 31

1,523 connections in 24 days needs ~63 net accepted a day. Safe sending volume (~15/day to avoid platform restrictions) at a realistic accept rate gets you a fraction of that — see the reality check on the sprint tab. Keep sending at a sustainable pace and let it run into November rather than spiking volume to chase the date; a restricted account sets you back further than a missed deadline does.

Scripture is paced to finish three books, not sample thirty

30 chapters across John, Philippians and James at roughly 1.25/day, with the 2-chapter days absorbing slack so none of the three is left half-read on Nov 1. Breadth across the whole Bible this month would have meant finishing nothing — depth on three books, chosen deliberately, was the actual ask.

Push-ups are the constant, everything else rotates

50 push-ups is non-negotiable every day. The second slot varies — walk, squats-and-plank, or a lighter recovery set on review Sundays — for the same reason the learning tracks alternate: the same movement every single day gets skipped first when a day is tight, a rotating one holds attention.

The three habits are the floor, not the ceiling

Sleep, Duolingo and a GitHub commit are small and fast on purpose — they're what keeps the streaks alive and the rest of the day functional, not goals to optimize. Checking them off should take under five minutes combined most days; if any one of them starts taking longer, that's worth noticing on a review Sunday.

Checkboxes, the LinkedIn/X counts and everything else you check off all save locally in this browser only — they won't sync with the app's own progress.json, LinkedIn, X, GitHub, Duolingo or a Bible app, or follow you to another device.
